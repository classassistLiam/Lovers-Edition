EDITORIAL

What are we really celebrating?

Behind every relationship are two human beings trying to understand one another. Sometimes they get it right. Sometimes they learn. The healthiest relationships give both people room to grow.

Companionship

A relationship can begin with attraction, but companionship is what often makes it meaningful over time. It is having someone to call after a difficult day, someone to laugh with, someone whose presence makes ordinary moments feel less ordinary.

Emotional comfort

Men are often expected to be strong, financially responsible and emotionally controlled. Yet men also experience loneliness, fear, pressure and disappointment. A healthy relationship should allow a man to be human without making vulnerability a weakness.

Mutual respect

Love is not ownership. Partnership means both people have a voice, boundaries, responsibilities and the freedom to say yes or no. Respect has to travel in both directions.

MEN & RELATIONSHIPS

How the role of men has changed

Across generations, many cultures have placed particular expectations on men: provide, protect, work hard, remain strong and take responsibility for the household.

Those responsibilities can be meaningful. But modern relationships also remind us that partnership is bigger than financial provision. Men can cook. Men can listen. Men can comfort. Men can apologise. Men can ask for help. Men can celebrate their partner's achievements. Men can also say, "Today I am not okay."

The strongest form of masculinity is not domination. It is responsibility combined with self-control, empathy, honesty and respect.

"Being a good man in a relationship does not mean never making mistakes. It means taking responsibility for how your choices affect another person."
LOVE

Relationships need more than chemistry

Attraction can start a relationship. Communication, trust, friendship, accountability and shared values help people navigate what comes next.

Talk

Say what you mean instead of expecting your partner to read your mind.

Trust

Trust grows through consistent behaviour, honesty and keeping promises.

Friendship

Your partner should also be someone you genuinely enjoy spending time with.

Growth

People change. Healthy relationships make room for learning and growth.

INTIMACY & SEX

Sex should never replace communication.

Sex can be an important part of an adult relationship. For many couples it can express affection, attraction, playfulness and closeness.

But intimacy becomes healthier when both partners can communicate about boundaries, contraception, sexual health, expectations and consent without fear of ridicule or pressure.

Consent is not implied by being someone's boyfriend or girlfriend. It should be freely given, informed and capable of being withdrawn.

Comfort matters too. Sometimes intimacy means sex. Sometimes it means holding hands, sleeping beside someone, talking late into the night, sharing a meal or simply sitting together.

A healthier intimacy checklist

Both people genuinely want it.
Boundaries can be discussed openly.
Protection and sexual health are taken seriously.
No one is pressured because of love, money or relationship status.
Both partners can communicate what makes them comfortable.
RESPECT • RESPONSIBILITY • SAFETY

Real love does not need violence.

Today is also a chance to recognise men who deliberately choose not to use violence against their partners.

Not hitting someone is not a favour. It is a basic standard of respect. The same principle applies to threats, intimidation, controlling behaviour, sexual coercion, stalking and other forms of abuse.

A man can be angry without becoming violent. He can walk away from an argument, ask for space, seek counselling, talk to a trusted person or return to the conversation when both people are calmer.

And importantly, responsibility belongs to the person choosing abusive behaviour. A difficult argument, relationship problem or disagreement does not justify violence.

A message to men

You can be strong without being violent. You can be masculine without controlling someone. You can disagree without humiliating someone. You can leave an unhealthy situation without hurting anyone.

Choose self-control. Choose respect.
